Work at your own depth.

Isoline is a workspace built in layers. Talk on the top floor, flow on the middle one, and go quiet all the way down.

Book a day pass Walk the three floors

Most offices are built for interruption. We built ours like a well.

Three floors, each quieter than the one above it. You choose how far down to go each morning, and the building keeps everyone else at their own depth.

  • Shallow, level 3
  • Flow, level 2
  • Deep, level 1

Three depths. Pick one each morning.

Every floor has one colour and one rule about sound. The lower you sit, the deeper the colour and the quieter the room.

  • Level 3

    Shallow

    Calls, coffee and talk. The floor for starting the day, meeting people and coming back up after a long stretch.

  • Level 2

    Flow

    Long desks and low voices. Questions are whispered or written down, headphones are welcome, laptops stay open.

  • Level 1

    Deep

    Single carrels and sealed booths. Phones sleep in lockers by the stairs, and nobody taps a shoulder.

The building has a day of its own.

The floors open in order and the quiet moves through them. Here is a weekday, from the first key to the last light.

  1. 7:30
    Level 3

    Doors and coffee

    The top floor opens first. Say hello now; it is the loudest the building gets.

  2. 9:00
    Level 2

    Quiet hours begin

    Flow fills up. Voices drop to a whisper and the long desks go still.

  3. 10:30
    Level 1

    The deep block

    Phones go into lockers at the stairs. Booths are held for three hours, no knocking.

  4. 13:30
    Level 3

    Up for lunch

    One long table on the roof. Talk about anything except what you are working on.

  5. 15:00
    Level 1

    Second descent

    The afternoon block. Most members say this is when the real work happens.

  6. 18:30
    Level 3

    Surfacing

    Lights warm up, the roof opens, and on Thursdays someone talks about how they finished something.

  7. 21:00
    All levels

    Last light

    The building closes from the bottom up. Residents keep a key to their booth.

Rooms, in order of descent.

Four places to sit, listed the way you reach them: from the roof, down the stairs, to the carrels at the bottom.

  • Level 3

    The Roof

    Benches, a long lunch table and the only place in the building for phone calls. Open from spring to the first frost.

  • Level 2

    The Long Room

    Forty seats along two oak tables under west windows. Bring headphones; the afternoon light does the rest.

  • Between levels

    The Stairs

    The landing where you leave your phone and your voice. Lockers, water, and a bench for deciding how far down to go.

  • Level 1

    The Carrels

    Twenty-two white carrels and six sealed booths. One clock per room, no screens on the walls, nothing to look at but the work.

Three rules, posted at the stairs.

Each floor has exactly one. They get shorter as you go down.

  1. Level 3

    Talk is fine. Calls go up on the roof.

    Meetings, laughter and coffee orders all belong up here.

  2. Level 2

    Whisper, or write it down.

    Every desk has a notepad for questions.

  3. Level 1

    Phones sleep upstairs.

    Lockers wait on the landing.
