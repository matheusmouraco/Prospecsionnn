Dusk delivery

Cut at five, at your door by nine

Order by three and tonight's flowers leave with the evening. They travel best after the heat of the day, and rest best overnight in a cool house.

  1. 5 pm

    The last cut

    Our growers cut in the cool of the evening, when stems hold the most water. By half past five the buckets are on Whitaker Street.

  2. 6 pm

    On the bench

    One pair of hands makes each order, start to finish, wraps it in paper and writes the card in ink. Never cellophane, never foam.

  3. 7 pm

    The vans leave

    Two vans, eleven neighbourhoods, routes drawn that afternoon from the orders in hand. Midtown first, the islands last.

  4. 8 pm

    At your door

    We text ten minutes ahead. If nobody's home, the flowers wait in the shade with a jar of water, never on a hot step.

  5. 9 pm

    Open by morning

    Overnight in a cool house the flowers drink, settle and open. That is the whole idea: they look their best at breakfast.

A care column, answered by hand

Dear Marigold

dear marigold

The letter of 3 October

Re-cut them, darling

“My dahlias droop by the third day, whatever I do. Is it me?” Wilting, in Thunderbolt.

Dear Wilting, it isn't you, it's the cut. A stem starts to seal itself within hours, and a sealed stem drinks nothing, however full the vase. Every morning take a finger's width off each stem, at a slant, with a sharp knife rather than scissors, which crush.

Then give them a clean vase and cool water, up to a third of the stem, no higher; dahlias rot where leaves sit under water. Keep them out of the sun and away from the fruit bowl, and they'll give you a week. Cut them half open next time, too. They finish opening at home, which is the best part.

Marigold

Write to Marigold

  • 19 September

    The fridge is not your friend

    The fruit drawer breathes out a gas that ages a bouquet by days. Keep flowers cool, not cold, and well away from the apples.

  • 5 September

    Wash the vase, don't rinse it

    A film you cannot see feeds the bacteria that block a stem. Hot water, a drop of soap and a bottle brush, every time.

Questions

Before you order

Anything else, call the shop. A person answers, usually the one holding the snips.

How late can I order for tonight?

Until three in the afternoon, Tuesday to Saturday. After that, call the shop: if a van has room, we'll say so.

Where do you deliver?

Eleven neighbourhoods, from the Historic District to Ardsley Park, Thunderbolt and the Southside, and Tybee and the islands on Fridays. Delivery is $12, free on standing orders.

Can I choose the flowers?

Give us a colour, a mood and a size and we'll choose from the bench. If you want something particular, ask, and we'll tell you honestly whether the fields have it this week.

How long will they last?

Five to ten days, depending on the stems. Every order comes with a care card: re-cut and change the water every second day.

How far ahead should we book a wedding?

Six months for April to June and October, three months otherwise. We take two weddings a weekend at most, so each gets the whole team.

Can I collect from the shop instead?

Yes, any time from eleven to nine. Collection orders are ready an hour after you place them.

Do you use floral foam?

Never. We build on chicken wire, pin frogs and moss, and reuse or compost all of it.

Do you send sympathy flowers?

Yes, quietly and the same day. Call the shop and we'll deliver to a home, a chapel or a funeral home before the evening.
