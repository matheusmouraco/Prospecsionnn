We build in the gaps a city leaves.

Midblock is an architecture office for plots under ten metres wide: the gap between two houses, the yard behind a shop, the corner nobody could use.

See the buildingsBring us a plot

A street takes a century to build and a second to see.

Before we draw a line, we film the plot’s street from first light to midnight and read it as one long exposure: where the sun reaches, who walks past, which colours the neighbours have worn for decades. Then we design the building the gap was waiting for.

Read the register

We read a street for a whole day

A slit camera records one vertical line of the street from first light to midnight. Sun, shade, traffic and the colour of a neighbour’s door all end up in one long image. That image is our brief.

  1. First light
  2. Noon
  3. Dusk
  4. Midnight

  1. Scan

    05:40 · first light

    We fix a camera across the street from the plot and record a single vertical line of it until midnight.

  2. Read

    12:10 · noon

    The long image shows where the sun reaches the ground, how long the shade lies and who uses the pavement at which hour.

  3. Draw

    19:30 · dusk

    We draw the building to the street’s own heights and colours, and give the sun back to the windows that lose it.

  4. Build

    23:55 · midnight

    Small sites mean small crews: one contractor, short sections, and a street that stays open while we work.

Questions we hear on site

The short answers. The long ones we give on the pavement, in front of your plot.

How narrow is too narrow?

We have built on 2.9 metres. Below about 2.4 metres a stair and a room no longer fit side by side, so we look at the plot behind or above instead.

What does a first visit cost?

Nothing. We walk the plot with you and tell you what the rules allow; only then do we quote for a scan and a feasibility study.

Do you only work in this city?

Most of our buildings are within an hour of the studio, because we want to be on site every week. We take on one plot further away each year.

Who owns the plots on the register?

Private owners, the council and two housing associations. We list a plot only when its owner agrees, and we never pass on their details.

How long from scan to keys?

About two years: three months of scan and design, nine to twelve months of planning, then seven to ten months on site.
